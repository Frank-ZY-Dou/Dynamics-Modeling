Design note

SimReady Gate

An agentic, verifier-in-the-loop layer that makes language-generated scenes penetration-free and simulation-ready. A small domain-specific language is the spine: text becomes a typed program of bodies, constraints and objectives, which compiles to S4R's repair QP, to the verification predicates, and to the simulator export. RoboLab (USD, Isaac Lab) and RoboCasa (MJCF, MuJoCo) are the first two asset worlds.

1. What the two asset worlds do today, and where they break

RoboLab

The /robolab-scenegen skill turns a sentence into placement predicates (place-on-base, left-of, place-on, place-in, random-rot), solves them, writes a .usda, and then runs settle_scenes.py. Reading the solver sources, none of the three layers ever looks at a mesh:

  • SpatialSolver is a 2D disc test. Each object is a circle of radius max(w,d)/2 + margin; a pair collides when the centre distance is below the radius sum. Orientation is ignored, so random-rot on an elongated object is unchecked. The margin starts at 5 cm and drops to 1.2 cm for 18+ objects, then is relaxed ×1.25, ×1.5, ×2.0 when the solver fails.
  • PhysicalSolver is dimension-based. Stacking sets z = support_top + h/2 + 1 mm; containment is an ellipse test on bounding-box dims. A bowl or mug that "contains" an object by dims can still intersect it at the mesh, which is exactly the nested-cavity regime where repair is hardest.
  • Settling is used as the repair. settle_scenes.py runs a fixed 300 steps (~5 s), has no velocity threshold, and keeps whatever rests at the end, including objects that fell or still interpenetrate.

RoboCasa

RoboCasa composes 2,500+ kitchen scenes from fixtures and 3,200+ objects (Objaverse and AI-generated, as MJCF models with mesh geoms) on robosuite/MuJoCo. Objects are placed by robosuite's UniformRandomSampler, whose validity test is a bounding cylinder: a candidate is rejected when the XY centre distance is below the sum of the two horizontal_radius values and the z-extents overlap; after 5,000 rejected samples it raises RandomizationError("Cannot place all objects"). There is no mesh-level check and no settle. (Its validity test for MJCF objects is a separating-axis test on the rotated reg_bbox boxes plus a corner-in-region test; the cylinder is only the fallback for other object types.) Measured on its AI-generated objects with RoboCasa's own test (Section 10), the box test is conservative rather than leaky: its accepted layouts do not interpenetrate, and its only cost is capacity in tight regions (a 0.3 m square at ten or more objects), where a mesh-aware repair still places every object; it never checks whether an object is stable once placed.

Why settling cannot be the repair. Stepping a physics engine from a deeply interpenetrating state produces violent contact impulses. In the S4R engine-initialization study (App. A.15), raw overlapping layouts reached peak body speeds of 7.8 m/s in MuJoCo, 12.5 m/s in PyBullet and 230.6 m/s in Isaac Gym (PhysX); the same layouts after S4R stayed at rest. A settled RoboLab scene therefore either exploded quietly or carries residual penetration that corrupts object_on_top, object_inside and object_upright at episode reset.

2. Principles

  1. Verifier in the loop. No language model ever decides whether geometry is feasible. Every physical claim passes a deterministic gate; the agent orchestrates, routes failures and holds the semantic intent.
  2. Repair is a tool, not a prayer. S4R resolves overlaps with minimal displacement; settling only confirms that a certified state stays at rest in the target simulator.
  3. Preserve intent. After repair, the task predicates are re-evaluated; the agent re-plans only when a relation actually broke.
  4. Certificate and provenance. A scene enters the benchmark with a manifest: asset hashes, proxy parameters, penetration count, minimum gap, settle statistics, repair displacement, tool versions. Benchmarks live or die on reproducibility.

3. The DSL is the spine

The intent a person expresses in words ("a mug upright on the counter, inside the tray, at least 2 cm from the edge, left of the bowl") is exactly the information the repair QP, the verifier and the exporter all need, and today each of them re-derives it separately. The DSL makes it one typed program with three layers:

scene
bodies with a mesh source (USD payload, MJCF mesh, GLB), pose, fixed or free, collision-proxy spec, mass and friction, semantic tags (container, support, up axis). Importers from .usda and MJCF; exporters back.
program
constraints and objectives over bodies. Each statement has a compile target in the per-step S4R QP and an evaluator predicate, so what the QP enforces and what the gate checks are the same object.
gate spec
which gates run, thresholds (minimum gap, settle speed and displacement), budgets, and the certificate fields.

text2function is the agent's only creative act: natural language → a program in this DSL, produced under a JSON schema, type-checked against the scene (every body exists, containers are tagged as such), with defaults recorded when the text is silent. The compiler is deterministic. Ambiguity is resolved by asking or by a logged default, never by the model guessing at geometry.

scene kitchen_counter
  fixed   counter   : support, up=+z, mesh=fixtures/counter.xml
  fixed   tray      : container, mesh=objs/tray/model.xml
  body    mug       : mesh=objs/mug/model.xml
  body    bowl      : mesh=objs/bowl/model.xml

program
  no_penetration(*, margin=0.02)          # contact rows, generated by detection
  on_support(mug, counter)  upright(mug)  # support-plane equality + roll/pitch homotopy
  on_support(bowl, counter) upright(bowl)
  inside(mug, tray)                       # half-spaces of the tray cavity
  left_of(mug, bowl, axis=y, gap>=0.05)   # linear row in Δp
  within(*, counter.top, inset=0.02)      # table bounds, 2 cm from the edge
  minimize displacement(*)                # S4R's minimum-norm objective
  prefer(bowl, pose=intent, w=0.1)        # soft proximal to the described layout

gate
  G2: min_gap >= 1e-3   G5: v_max <= 0.05, dx <= 0.01
  certify: hashes, seeds, versions

6. The agent

The agent is a state machine that calls tools; the gates are code. What it owns is the decisions that numbers cannot make.

tools
text2dsl · compile_dsl · check_asset · repair_asset · evaluate_layout · s4r_repair(program, fixed=[…]) · verify_predicates · settle_and_measure · export(usda | mjcf)
routes
overlap → S4R · repair residual → shrink intent · nested container → semantic decision · asset defect → repair or replace · settle motion → mass, friction or support fix
VLM scope
semantic plausibility only (does the layout match the description). Screenshots cannot reveal mesh-level penetration; the VLM never judges geometry.
determinism
seeds, hashes and certificates on every artifact; the same request reproduces the same scene

7. Where it plugs in

RoboLab

  1. skills/robolab-scenegen step 3 ("offer to reduce object count") becomes the G2→G3→G4 loop.
  2. settle_scenes.py gains a pre-hook (G2/G3) and a post-measure (G5: peak speed, displacement, thresholds).
  3. assets/objects/_utils/ gains the admission script (G0) that emits an asset certificate; it also admits generated assets.
  4. Task reset (InitialStateCfg sampling) gains G6.

Data path. Parse the .usda payload references, read each object's mesh with usd-core (pip, no Isaac), build the scene layer with the scene transforms, verify, repair, and write the corrected xformOp:translate / xformOp:orient back. Only G5 touches Isaac Sim.

RoboCasa

  1. A placement hook after UniformRandomSampler (or a drop-in sampler) runs G2→G3 on the sampled poses before the MuJoCo model is compiled.
  2. The MJCF importer reads fixture and object <body> poses and mesh geoms; the exporter writes pos / quat back.
  3. G5 uses mujoco directly (no robosuite dependency) to settle and measure.
  4. AI-generated objects go through G0 admission first; this is where generated-asset defects show up most.

8. Evaluation

The first experiment is diagnostic and needs no new method: run /robolab-scenegen and RoboCasa's task samplers on fixed prompt and seed sets, and measure, before and after the gate,

  • the fraction of generated scenes with mesh-level penetration, and the penetration count per scene;
  • settle statistics: peak speed, displacement, objects leaving the table;
  • the fraction of episode resets whose task predicates are already false or already true at t = 0;
  • the variance this induces in policy evaluation across seeds.

The framing is certified simulation-ready scene synthesis: a certificate specification, repair as a tool inside an agent loop, and the measured effect on a public manipulation benchmark. Generated-asset admission ties it to the HY3D-Bench line of the S4R paper.

9. Plan and boundaries

  1. Scene layer + DSL v0 — importers for .usda and MJCF, the typed program with its compile rules for the statements above; text2function comes after the compiler is trusted.
  2. G2 on both worlds — evaluator reads RoboLab and RoboCasa scenes; report penetration on the shipped scenes and on fresh generated layouts.
  3. G3 upright S4R from the DSL — compiled rows, fixtures fixed, write back; verify predicates.
  4. Diagnostic paper-grade numbers — the before/after table of Section 7.
  5. Agent loop and G0 admission — the agent skill wrapper, failure routing, certificates.
  6. G5/G6 with Isaac — measured settle, per-reset gate.

10. Results (corrected 2026-09-08 after an independent review pass)

What the review changed. An independent review of the repair core, the gates, the DSL and CLI, the asset I/O and the experiments against their claims found no fabricated numbers, but the first pass had been unfair to both baselines and had produced claims that do not survive: the RoboLab replica seated objects on the wrong fixture (the robot's franka_table instead of the table_oak its skill uses), so the "bounds bug", the edge failures and the "orange rolls off the table" route were artifacts; its solver bounds were 35 % narrower than the skill's; layouts were not seeded; RoboLab's front/back convention was inverted in the DSL; the RoboCasa sampler replica used a bounding cylinder where RoboCasa tests rotated reg_bbox boxes; raw layouts were compared hovering against re-seated repaired ones; the MJCF loader ignored refquat; and the USD writer silently discarded the repair. All of it is fixed (see docs/DIAGNOSTIC_2026-09-08.md), the experiments were re-run on seeded, tracked layouts, and the withdrawn claims are listed there.

The system runs end to end on real assets: USD and MJCF importers (MJCF objects compiled by MuJoCo itself), G2 on a probed, containment-aware FCL evaluator, the upright S4R repair with DSL rows, text-to-program (schema-constrained JSON through the Anthropic SDK backend or the agent skill), G5 as a measured MuJoCo settle with a per-body verdict, and certificates with provenance. Numbers below come from experiments/ and the tracked results/*.json.

RoboLab, shipped library (68 scenes, already physics-settled by their pipeline)

  • Object–object mesh penetration in 26 / 68 scenes (≥ 1 mm in 12; the largest score 9.9 mm, keyboard/smartphone in workdesk_snacks). Sub-millimetre resting penetration into fixtures in 64 / 68 (median 0.27 mm). A body wholly inside another, invisible to any surface test, in 4 scenes (cutlery inside racks and plates, a clamp inside a bin). 30 / 68 scenes hold a body that nothing supports from below.
  • Reading: the library is clean in proxy space and not in mesh space — the two-space G2 of Section 5, with a containment test, is not a nicety. Scores are FCL clip lengths: only their sign is exact.

RoboLab, pre-settle layouts from its own SpatialSolver (N ∈ {4,6,8,10} × 3 seeds, staged in its base scene)

  • With the skill's own bounds the disc solver accepts 5 / 12 layouts (all at N = 4, one each at N = 6 and 8, none at N = 10). Accepted layouts carry no mesh penetration once seated; the rejected ones carry 1–7 penetrating pairs, and the skill's answer to them is "reduce the object count".
